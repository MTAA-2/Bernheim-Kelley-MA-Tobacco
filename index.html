About The Massachusetts Tobacco Lawsuit

Big Tobacco Knew the Truth - and Hid It.

For decades, cigarette manufacturers knew smoking caused deadly diseases and that nicotine was addictive. Rather than warn the public, they worked together to conceal the dangers, dispute the science, and keep Americans smoking.


They Targeted Children and Misled Smokers.

Tobacco companies distributed free cigarette samples to children and teenagers, deliberately recruited young smokers, and marketed “light” and “ultralight” cigarettes as safer alternatives—even though they were not. Millions became addicted before they understood the risks, and many later developed lung cancer, COPD, and other devastating diseases.

You Still Have the Right to Sue.

Massachusetts law allows eligible smokers and families of deceased smokers to pursue individual lawsuits against cigarette manufacturers. The Master Settlement Agreement did not take away those rights. Even if you started smoking decades ago, knew smoking was dangerous, or quit years before your diagnosis, you may still have a case.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I Still Sue a Cigarette Company in Massachusetts?

Yes. Massachusetts smokers who develop lung cancer, COPD, or other serious smoking-related illnesses may have the right to bring individual lawsuits against cigarette manufacturers. Families of smokers who have died may also have claims.

Didn't the Tobacco Companies Already Settle These Cases?

No. The 1998 Master Settlement Agreement resolved claims brought by state governments. It did not eliminate the rights of individual smokers and their families to pursue their own lawsuits.

What If I Knew Smoking Was Dangerous?

You may still have a case. Knowing that smoking posed health risks does not automatically prevent someone from bringing a lawsuit against a cigarette manufacturer. Every case is different, and your legal rights depend on the particular facts and circumstances.

What If I Started Smoking as a Child or Teenager??

Starting as a minor can be an important part of your case. For decades, tobacco companies marketed cigarettes to young people, distributed free samples, and worked to create generations of nicotine-dependent customers. Many smokers became addicted before they fully understood the consequences.

What do I have to prove to win?

Two things: (1) actual harm — a serious health condition like cancer or COPD caused by smoking; and (2) breach — that the tobacco company breached its duty to consumers, whether through a defective product, negligence, or intentional wrongdoing.

What If I Smoked “Light” or “Ultralight” Cigarettes?

You may have a claim. Tobacco companies marketed these cigarettes in ways that led consumers to believe they were safer than regular cigarettes. Evidence that you relied on those representations may be important to your case.

What If I Quit Smoking Years Ago?

You may still qualify. Many smoking-related diseases develop years or decades after a person stops smoking. Quitting does not automatically prevent you from pursuing a claim against the cigarette manufacturers.

What If My Husband, Wife, or Parent Died from Smoking?

Eligible family members or representatives of a deceased smoker's estate may be able to bring a wrongful death or other legal claim. The circumstances of the death, the smoking history, and the applicable filing deadlines will determine whether a claim can proceed.
